\section{Introduction}
The Tor Project, commonly known as Tor, it is, if not the most, the preferred network overlay to provide 
an anonymous communication network. In 2003 the developers introduced an anonymity mechanism to protect
the service providers, onion services, on the Tor network, that was later deployed in 2004 as the version 2 (V2)
of the onion services. (TODO: USE REF 1 OF DOC)

Each onion service generates an address when started, a domain name, used by Tor clients to access the service.
To achieve a communication between a client and an onion service a two-way anonymous communication is established
over a 6-hop circuit, beaing each hop a Tor node. Nonetheless, Tor deprecates the version 2 protocol
in 2021 (TODO: USE REF 2 OF DOC), and migrates to the third version of onion services (V3) due to the 
different issues caused by the V2 protocol. Among these issues are included, (TODO: INCLUDE PROBLEMS). The
newly deployed V3 protocol assesses these issues including avoiding direct exposure of onion services to
Hidden Service Directories, now onward referred as HSDirs, which may abuse of onion services.

To understand the current situation of HSDirs security, a set of onion services, consisting of personal onion services static
and ephemeral, and third party onion services, is put under a 30-days public data gathering of HSDirs on the network hosting the 
onion services descriptors, and perform a comprehensive analysis to shed light on the current security regarding
hosting HSDirs (Tor relays) on the Tor network.
